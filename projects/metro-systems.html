A new way to study urban infrastructure

Urban transit systems vary enormously in their histories, size, and the cities they serve. This project asks how those systems are documented in English Wikipedia—and how article attention and structure might relate to characteristics of the systems themselves.

The project combines an enumerated corpus of articles with revision records, pageview histories, article text, and manually curated system-level metadata.

What counts as a metro-system article?

The population begins with the main system links in English Wikipedia’s List of metro systems ↗. The unit of analysis is a linked article about an individual system, not every page reachable through an article.

I normalize redirects and page titles, exclude disambiguation pages, and retain one article per enumerated system. This explicit population rule makes the project easier to audit and repeat.

Data & planned comparisons

The corpus was initially retrieved on November 2, 2026. It contains article text, up to 500 recent revisions per page, and daily pageviews for January 1 through December 31, 2025. Metadata include geography, opening year, system length, ridership, stations, and city population where available.

Interpretation & limitations

Wikipedia activity is a measure of online attention and participation, not the quality of transit service or the importance of a city. Differences in article coverage may reflect language, access, editorial interests, and data availability. The revision sample is also capped by the retrieval process, so results should not be described as complete editing histories.

Research direction

Combining metadata with a clearly defined online corpus can show patterns in what infrastructure receives public attention—and what may be overlooked.
